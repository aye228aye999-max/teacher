# AI Chatbots and Voice Agents for L2 (English) Speaking Practice — Evidence Notes 2020–2026

> Compiled 26 Sep 2026 to inform an AI voice English tutor (Russian-speaking adults, A2–B1, "understand but can't speak", ~20-min speech-to-speech lessons).
>
> **Evidence tiers used below:** **[A]** meta-analysis or large randomized trial · **[B]** controlled primary study (randomized or quasi-experimental with comparison group) · **[C]** single-group / very small / low-tier venue / perception-only · **[V]** vendor/company claim (not independently verified).

## 1. Measured effects of chatbots, voice assistants and LLM conversational agents on L2 speaking (meta-analyses, RCTs, quasi-experiments, 2020–2026)

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses converge on **medium pooled effects (g ≈ 0.48–0.65)** of chatbot/dialogue-system practice on L2 outcomes, and the only **speaking-specific meta-analysis finds g = .61** (Hou & Min, 2026); affective outcomes (lower anxiety, higher willingness to communicate) are the most consistently reported benefit, though two 2026 meta-analyses disagree on anxiety. The base is dominated by small, short quasi-experiments with publication bias and almost no delayed post-tests, so **short-term gains are well supported; durable, real-world transfer is not yet demonstrated.**

### Cited Findings

#### [A] Meta-analyses (newest first)
- **[2026] Hou, Z. & Min, S. — "Dialogue-based computer-assisted language learning systems for second language speaking development: A three-level meta-analysis," *ReCALL* 38(1), Jan 2026.** The only speaking-specific meta-analysis found: 16 studies, 89 effect sizes, 13 moderators tested; **overall g = .61** (moderate) on L2 speaking development. **Significant moderators:** *type of system*, *system meaning constraint*, *system modality*. **Not significant:** education stage, **L2 proficiency**, learning location, **corrective feedback**, **length of intervention**, type of interaction, measure, key assessment component. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/dialoguebased-computerassisted-language-learning-systems-for-second-language-speaking-development-a-threelevel-metaanalysis/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B); [ReCALL 38(1) issue](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/issue/CCD0001B89698B40CC9CEAC9D43790B8). An **ADDENDUM** was later published (content not retrieved) — [Cambridge Core addendum](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/dialoguebased-computerassisted-language-learning-systems-for-second-language-speaking-development-a-threelevel-metaanalysis-addendum/8E7C14174B9131434BAB44EE99CA7778)
- **[2026, June] "The effects of generative AI on foreign language emotions: A meta-analysis," *Acta Psychologica*.** 23 experimental/quasi-experimental studies, 43 effect sizes, N = 3,461; three-level random-effects model: **g = 0.788, p < .001** in favour of GenAI-assisted instruction vs traditional instruction. Emotion valence moderated: **larger improvements for negative emotions (anxiety, boredom)** than for positive emotions (enjoyment). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691826011376); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42361459/)
- **[2026] "Artificial intelligence and academic anxiety? a meta-analysis," *BMC Psychology*.** Search summary: a meta-analysis found **AI did not produce a statistically significant reduction in foreign language anxiety (g = −0.59, p = 0.12)**; some individual studies even reported slight *increases* in FL anxiety among university students. *(attribution uncertain — snippet did not name the paper explicitly; this was the meta-analysis in the result set.)* — [BMC Psychology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-026-05018-y). **Conflicts with** the Acta Psychologica result above.
- **[2026] "How do pedagogical approaches affect the impact of chatbots on learning performance? A meta-analysis and research synthesis," *Educational Research Review*** (cross-subject, not language-only): 56 studies, 96 effect sizes, robust variance estimation; concludes "the effectiveness of chatbot-based interventions depends not only on technological affordances but also on how chatbots are pedagogically integrated"; examined interactions with subject area, educational level, chatbot type, chatbot role and intervention duration. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X26000229)
- **[2025] Li, M., Wang, Y. & Yang, X. — "Can Generative AI Chatbots Promote Second Language Acquisition? Significant moderators: mobility of access, **chatbot modality**, whether generative AI was involved, and the **nature of the comparison group**. The same result set reported "more positive effects of shorter intervention durations, and the smallest effect size … for long durations" *(attribution to Lyu et al. likely but uncertain)*. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390850220_Effectiveness_of_Chatbots_in_Improving_Language_Learning_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Comparative_Studies)

#### [A/V] Largest randomized trial of an AI conversation feature (vendor-run; full details in Q4)
- **[2025] Duolingo Video Call RCT:** 567 Japanese learners at CEFR B1.1 randomized; ≥2 AI video calls/day for 30 days vs ≥1 regular lesson/day; Versant by Pearson speaking test; Video Call group significantly outperformed control on speaking. — [Duolingo whitepaper](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/Duolingo_whitepaper_language_video_call_improves_speaking_2025.pdf)

#### [B] Controlled primary studies (newest first)
- **[2026] "What Changes When the Interlocutor Is an AI? Interactional Fluency and Linguistic Uptake in L2 Spoken Dialogue" (EDM 2026 / arXiv 2606.22225).** 78 university learners of **German** at four sites did a counterbalanced spot-the-difference task with a human peer and with a real-time AI partner. Human dialogue was "faster and more balanced, with many short turns"; AI dialogue "resembled supported monologue, with fewer, longer turns, reduced learner floor share, and greater within-turn fluency." The AI's "verbose, syntactically regular input was associated with greater short-term uptake and stronger syntactic priming" (controlling for input volume). Attitudes toward AI improved after the task; **satisfaction was predicted by production fluency rather than uptake.** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22225); [EDM 2026 proceedings](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2026/proceedings/2026.EDM.full-papers.210/index.html)

### Inferences
- **Plan around g ≈ 0.5–0.6 for proximal, immediate outcomes, and assume the durable real-world effect is smaller.** Three findings point that way: publication bias (Laun & Wolff), larger effects in very short interventions (Li et al.; Lyu et al.), and moderation by comparison-group type (Lyu et al.). Marketing should not promise "+1 CEFR level in X weeks" on the strength of this literature.
- **The evidence fits a supplementary speaking-practice product best.** Effects are largest when AI practice is compared with *no* speaking practice, and for self-studying Russian-speaking adults who "understand but can't speak" that is the realistic counterfactual. The evidence is weakest for claims that AI replaces human teachers.
- **The most consistent gains are affective and fluency/complexity-related.** These include higher WTC, lower anxiety, and more fluency and syntactic complexity. **Accuracy gains are weakest and least consistent** (Iran 2026 CAF study; the app pilot in Q4 showed minimal lexical/grammatical change). This fits the target profile, whose bottleneck is fluency and willingness to speak, not knowledge.
- **Validate outcomes with human raters.** Many studies used automated scorers (Speechace, Versant). The product should check its own outcomes with human-rated speaking samples and a delayed post-test.

## 2. Design features that drive results (feedback, task structure/goals, voice vs text, human-likeness/persona, scaffolding/level adaptation, session length, role-play)

### Takeaway
Four features have the best support:
- **voice or mixed voice+text interaction**;
- **structured, task-focused, goal-oriented dialogue** rather than open chat;
- **maximising the learner's own spoken output**;
- **feedback designed to produce learner repair (uptake)**. Merely having corrective feedback did *not* moderate speaking gains.

Human-like embodiment appears to add affective benefits (WTC, anxiety). Direct evidence on session length, role-play types and persona is thin or absent.

### Cited Findings

#### Modality — voice vs text
- **Hou & Min (2026):** system modality was a significant moderator of speaking gains. The search summary reports that "dialogue systems with mixed system modality integrating voice, text, and additional facets like VR yielded a large and significant effect," and that "while learners prefer voice chatting over text chatting, a mixed written and spoken interface can increase the intelligibility of the interaction and thus facilitate optimal communication." *(attribution uncertain — snippet-level)* — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/dialoguebased-computerassisted-language-learning-systems-for-second-language-speaking-development-a-threelevel-metaanalysis/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B)
- **Voice input as a moderator.** A summary of the chatbot meta-analyses states that "studies that involved chatbots with **voice input** function generated a larger effect size than those that did not support voice input," and that this was a significant moderator. *(attribution uncertain — likely Wang et al. 2025 RER or Lyu et al. 2025)* — [Wang et al., RER (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381447647_Does_Chatting_with_Chatbots_Improve_Language_Learning_Performance_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Chatbot-Assisted_Language_Learning); [Lyu et al., IJAL](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668)
- **Lyu et al. (2025):** chatbot modality was a significant moderator; direction not captured. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668)
- **Contradiction:** Laun & Wolff (2025), across all school subjects, reported that **text-based chatbots outperformed speech-based ones**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608025000226)
- **The cost of voice.** It makes interaction feel more authentic but "introduces higher speech-recognition difficulties, especially for young and non-native speakers." Low intelligibility of learner utterances due to speech-recognition problems was "the most commonly reported problem" in the chatbot review literature. *(attribution uncertain — e.g., the ReCALL 2010–2024 meta-synthesis in the same result set)* — [ReCALL meta-synthesis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/use-of-artificially-intelligent-chatbots-in-english-language-learning-a-systematic-metasynthesis-study-of-articles-published-between-2010-and-2024/118654390007CCA33DEAD142D45C8D4F)

#### Structure, goals and task design
- **[2026] Educational Research Review meta-analysis (56 studies):** effectiveness "depends not only on technological affordances but also on how chatbots are pedagogically integrated." — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X26000229)
- **Related chatbot-learning meta-analysis** *(attribution uncertain — probably JRTE, "The effect of chatbots on learning: a meta-analysis of empirical research")*:
  - It found four significant moderators: type of instruction in the comparison group, experimental duration, chatbot type, and **chatbot tasks**.
  - Effect sizes were highest when the comparison group had no specific support, when the experiment lasted one session, and when chatbots were **task-focused**.
  - Chatbots giving **specific, structured support** yielded the greatest positive effects, compared with general or unspecific assistance.
  - — [T&F JRTE](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15391523.2023.2255698)
- **Hou & Min (2026):** *system meaning constraint* was a significant moderator of speaking gains. This is the degree to which dialogue meaning is constrained, e.g. scripted or goal-bound vs open. Direction not captured. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/dialoguebased-computerassisted-language-learning-systems-for-second-language-speaking-development-a-threelevel-metaanalysis/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B)
- **[2023] Jeon, J., Lee, S. & Choe, H. — "Beyond ChatGPT: A conceptual framework and systematic review of speech-recognition chatbots for language learning," *Computers & Education* 206, 104898.** Identifies 8 chatbot types and 12 affordances, and names **goal-orientation, embodiment and multimodality** as the key design dimensions for future R&D. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131523001756)
- **Taiwan BOPPPS study (2025):** structured stage-based ChatGPT integration vs unstructured AI practice, 18 weeks, n = 44 vs 45 (see Q1). — [ScienceDirect/IGI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2155687325000070)
- **Duolingo:** Video Call is described as "structured LLM conversations"; engineering details not retrieved. — [ZenML LLMOps database](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/structured-llm-conversations-for-language-learning-video-calls); [Duolingo blog: the AI behind Video Call](https://blog.duolingo.com/ai-and-video-call/)

  - "Narrative Suggestion: An Implicit Corrective Feedback Method for Foreign Language Learning with Role-Playing AI Chatbots" — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-19687-3_22)

#### Human-likeness, persona, embodiment, voice qualities
- **[2024, System]** Only the **avatar + voice + text** chatbot group (D-ID Agent) was reported to significantly raise WTC and self-perceived competence and to lower speaking anxiety vs control. The snippet did not report significant effects for the voice/text chatbot without an avatar (Typebot). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X24003154)
- **Bibauw et al. (2022)** included agent embodiment as a moderator; the result was not captured. — [LLT](https://www.lltjournal.org/item/10125-73488/)
- **[2026] Qualitative study (Frontiers in Psychology / PMC):** voice interaction shaped learners' sense of **safety**. "The AI's steady pacing and responsiveness created a sense of being attended to," and voice lets learners "manage time, hesitation, and reformulation without fear of evaluation." *(attribution uncertain)* — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13050954/); [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1786617/full)
- **[2026, CHI] Voice chatbots in Indian schools:** stakeholders wanted "a slower, Indian-accented audio channel and low-friction voice input" and "more intelligible speech output for non-native learners." — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791266); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19304)
- Persona work found but not extracted: "AI Twin: Enhancing ESL Speaking Practice through AI Self-Clones of a Better Me" (2026). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11103)

#### Scaffolding and level adaptation
- **Hou & Min (2026):** **L2 proficiency was *not* a significant moderator**; effects held across levels. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/dialoguebased-computerassisted-language-learning-systems-for-second-language-speaking-development-a-threelevel-metaanalysis/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B)
- **[2026] Pilot of 6 AI conversation apps:** advanced learners gained in fluency and pronunciation, but **intermediate learners improved only in pronunciation**, with minimal lexical and grammatical change (statistics in Q4). — [ScienceDirect/IGI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2155709826000137)
- **[2025] Ace-CEFR dataset:** built for automated evaluation of the linguistic difficulty of conversational texts for LLM applications. Its labels "reflect the productive rather than receptive challenge," supporting "output-adjustable" chatbots that deliver level-appropriate practice. — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14046); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392765479_Ace-CEFR_--_A_Dataset_for_Automated_Evaluation_of_the_Linguistic_Difficulty_of_Conversational_Texts_for_LLM_Applications)
- Work on controlling the difficulty of LLM output in conversation (findings not extracted): "Toward Beginner-Friendly LLMs for Language Learning: Controlling Difficulty in Conversation" (2025). — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04072)

#### Duration, dose, spacing (conflicting evidence)
- **Findings on intervention duration conflict:**
  - **Shorter is better:** Lyu et al. 2025, with the smallest effects for long durations *(attribution uncertain)* — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668). Li et al. 2025 found a large effect for 1–7 days — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.70060).
  - **Longer is better:** Laun & Wolff 2025, across all subjects — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608025000226).
  - **No moderation:** length was not significant for speaking in Hou & Min 2026 — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/dialoguebased-computerassisted-language-learning-systems-for-second-language-speaking-development-a-threelevel-metaanalysis/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B).
- **Duolingo's effective dose:** ≥2 Video Calls per day for 30 days. — [Duolingo whitepaper](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/Duolingo_whitepaper_language_video_call_improves_speaking_2025.pdf)
- **Spacing (pre-2020):** Fryer's follow-up research found that the drop in interest in the chatbot partner "does see a small, but significant rebound five months later," suggesting that **spaced** chatbot use may support interest. *(attribution uncertain — Fryer, Nakao & Thompson 2019 or a related Fryer paper)* — [ScienceDirect, Computers in Human Behavior 93](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563218306095)

#### Individual vs paired use
- **[2024, C&E, children]** Individual and paired interaction with a GenAI chatbot had similarly positive effects on speaking. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S036013152400126X)

### Inferences
- **Voice first, with text support.** Offer a live transcript or captions and a "what I heard" view as scaffolding for A2 learners. This mirrors the "mixed modality" pattern that performed best in Hou & Min, and it doubles as an ASR-error safeguard (see Q3).
- **Give every lesson a concrete communicative goal and task, not free chat.** Examples: an information gap, a role-play with a success condition, spot-the-difference, a problem to solve. The best-supported moderators favour task-focused, structured support. For "understand but can't speak" learners, structure also reduces the "what do I say?" freeze.
- **Protect learner talk time.** Real-time AI partners drift into "supported monologue".
  - Hard-limit tutor turns in the prompt (e.g., 1–2 short sentences at A2) and end most turns with an open question or prompt.
  - Log **learner talk ratio and words spoken per lesson** as primary KPIs (supported by the Duolingo words-spoken correlation).
  - Keep AI input syntactically regular and rich, which drives uptake and priming, but short.
- **Don't optimise for satisfaction.** In the 2026 interlocutor study, satisfaction tracked how fluent learners felt, not what they took up. Satisfaction or "fun" ratings are not proxies for learning.
- **Feedback: recast plus elicitation of self-repair, and log uptake.** The mere presence of CF did not moderate speaking effects, but uptake after recasts predicted retention (LLT 2024). Keep corrections sparse and minimal-edit (see the over-correction evidence in Q3).
- **Make level control explicit.** Constrain vocabulary and syntax to the target CEFR band (Ace-CEFR-style checks) and slow the TTS rate for A2. Intermediate learners showed little lexical or grammatical change from free conversation apps, so targeted recycling of lesson language is likely needed.
- **Session length.** A ~20-minute session is plausible but **not validated by any study found**. Distribute practice across days rather than massing it, and vary task types to counter novelty decay.

### Gaps
- No experimental evidence on optimal **session length** (e.g., 10 vs 20 vs 30 minutes) for AI speaking practice.
- No head-to-head comparisons of **role-play scenario types** or **persona designs** (teacher-like vs peer-like vs celebrity) for L2 speaking.
- **Feedback timing** (in-conversation vs post-session summary) was not compared in any retrieved AI-speaking study.
- No L2-learning comparisons of **native speech-to-speech models** (Gemini Live, OpenAI Realtime) vs cascaded ASR→LLM→TTS pipelines.
- The direction of the "type of system", "meaning constraint" and "modality" moderators in Hou & Min (2026) was not retrieved.

## 3. Known limitations and pitfalls (ASR on accented speech, over-/wrong correction, hallucinated rules, novelty effects, engagement decline, lack of delayed post-tests)

### Takeaway
The main risks are:
- ASR errors on accented, disfluent L2 speech, which are worse in conversation than in read speech;
- LLM **over-correction**: fluency rewrites instead of minimal edits;
- feedback that learners perceive as "hallucinated" or irrelevant;
- a well-documented **novelty effect**: interest in chatbot partners falls after the first sessions;
- AI dialogue that is structurally unlike human dialogue: fewer, longer turns and less learner floor;
- a research base inflated by publication bias and short, immediate-post-test designs.

### Cited Findings

#### ASR on accented / L2 speech
- **[2024] "Evaluating OpenAI's Whisper ASR: Performance analysis across diverse accents and speaker traits," *JASA Express Letters* 4(2), 025206.** Accuracy was higher for native than non-native accents, and higher for **read speech than conversational speech**. Word error rate was associated with speaker sex, L1 typology and L2 proficiency. — [AIP](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jel/article/4/2/025206/3267247/Evaluating-OpenAI-s-Whisper-ASR-Performance); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38391582/)
- **[2025] "Automatic Speech Recognition for Non-Native English: Accuracy and Disfluency Handling" (arXiv 2503.06924).**
  - Tested on L2-ARCTIC speakers with Arabic, Chinese, Hindi, Korean, Spanish and Vietnamese L1s.
  - On *read* speech Whisper was most accurate, with a mean Match Error Rate of 0.054.
  - Related results reported with it: French learners' read speech gave a mean WER of 0.132 (Whisper-large-v2); non-native Dutch children gave WER 0.248 on read speech vs **0.338 on dialogue speech**.
  - — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06924v2)
- **[2026, CHI] Voice chatbots for English speaking in Indian schools.**
  - "Nearly all students experienced ASR error issues," most often with proper nouns and Hindi loanwords. ASR "struggled with non-standard accents or imperfect pronunciation."
  - Engagement was "noticeably impeded by persistent technical barriers": ASR errors, microphone usability and network latency.
  - Speaking confidence still improved.
  - — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791266); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19304)
- **Chatbot review literature:** low intelligibility of learner utterances due to speech recognition was "the most commonly reported problem." *(attribution uncertain)* — [ReCALL meta-synthesis 2010–2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/use-of-artificially-intelligent-chatbots-in-english-language-learning-a-systematic-metasynthesis-study-of-articles-published-between-2010-and-2024/118654390007CCA33DEAD142D45C8D4F)

#### Over-correction and wrong corrections by LLMs
- **[2023] Fang et al. — "Is ChatGPT a Highly Fluent Grammatical Error Correction System? A Comprehensive Evaluation."** ChatGPT produces fewer under-corrections and mis-corrections but **more over-corrections**, and it does not adhere to the principle of minimal edits. It "cannot effectively correct agreement, coreference, tense errors across sentences, and cross-sentence boundary errors." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01746)
- **[2023–2024] Coyne et al. — "Analyzing the Performance of GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 in Grammatical Error Correction," and the review in "Prompting open-source and commercial language models for grammatical error correction of English learner text" (2024).**
  - GPT models set a new state of the art on JFLEG, where fluency rewrites are allowed.
  - They performed worse than the state of the art on CoNLL-14 and W&I+LOCNESS, which use minimal-edit annotation.
  - GPT models "have a tendency to over-correct and make extraneous fluency edits."
  - Prompting for minimal edits (Loem et al., 2023) gave "promising improvements."
  - — [arXiv 2303.14342](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.14342v2.pdf); [arXiv 2401.07702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.07702)
- **[2026]** Newer work tackles over-correction through prompting and batching (title: "Larger Context Window, Fewer Overcorrections: Optimizing Prompts and Batching for Minimal-Edit Grammatical Error Correction"); findings not extracted. — [arXiv 2609.10810](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10810)
- **Learner perception (2025–2026 perception study):** participants "anchored credibility in teacher assessment," trusting teacher feedback most, while "AI-generated feedback was sometimes perceived as irrelevant or hallucinated." *(attribution uncertain)* — [TESOL Union PDF, "The Trajectory of Learner Perceptions in Human and AI-…"](https://www.tesolunion.org/attachments/files/4ZTU2DNGI1BMMJK7MGVIDN2JKEZJNKDN2M37NJK27MGVJ6ZDHI4ZJQ0CMMVMDNDKZ3YZY57YTKY4NDFM7YJYX6M2MZ4LJQ24NZG2ENDAW4LJKZ.pdf)

#### Novelty effect and engagement decline
- **[2017, pre-2020 foundational] Fryer et al. — "Stimulating and sustaining interest in a language course: An experimental comparison of Chatbot and Human task partners," *Computers in Human Behavior* 75.**
  - Design: 12-week experiment with n = 122 foreign-language students.
  - Task interest **dropped after the first task with a chatbot partner** (a novelty effect); this did not happen with human partners.
  - Human-partner task interest predicted future course interest; chatbot-partner task interest did not.
  - — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563217303667); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317270477_Stimulating_and_sustaining_interest_in_a_language_course_An_experimental_comparison_of_Chatbot_and_Human_task_partners)
- **Follow-up:** there was a small but significant rebound in chatbot-partner interest 5 months later, which argues for spaced use. — [ScienceDirect, CHB 93](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563218306095)
- **Laun & Wolff (2025):** with no studies longer than 6 months, novelty effects cannot be assessed; publication bias is present (z = 7.931, p < .001). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608025000226)
- **Short interventions show larger effects** (Li et al. 2025: 1–7 days; Lyu et al. 2025: smallest effects for long durations). This is consistent with a novelty or measurement-proximity artefact. — [Li](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.70060); [Lyu](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668)
- **[2026] Discontinuance:** a study of why university students stop AI-mediated informal English learning (SEM/fsQCA); findings not extracted. — [arXiv 2604.27506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27506)

#### AI dialogue ≠ human dialogue (transfer risk)
- **[2026]** Human dialogue is "faster and more balanced, with many short turns"; AI dialogue is "supported monologue" with reduced learner floor share. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22225)
- **[2025–2026]** Perception research concludes that "a hybrid pedagogy, which sequences AI and human interaction to leverage their complementary strengths, is essential." *(attribution uncertain)* — [TESOL Union PDF](https://www.tesolunion.org/attachments/files/4ZTU2DNGI1BMMJK7MGVIDN2JKEZJNKDN2M37NJK27MGVJ6ZDHI4ZJQ0CMMVMDNDKZ3YZY57YTKY4NDFM7YJYX6M2MZ4LJQ24NZG2ENDAW4LJKZ.pdf)

#### Evidence-quality limitations
- **Publication bias:** Laun & Wolff (2025). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608025000226)
- **Regional clustering:** Li et al.'s (2025) large moderators include L1 **Indonesian**, which suggests that clusters of small regional studies can drive pooled estimates. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.70060)
- **Transfer:** long-term transfer to real-world communication "remains scarce." — [JALT CALL Journal](https://www.castledown.com/journals/jaltcall/article/view/jaltcall.v21n3.102884)
- **Automated outcome scoring is common:** Speechace in the Ecuador ChatGPT Voice study; Versant in Duolingo's RCT. — [Ciencia Latina](https://ciencialatina.org/index.php/cienciala/article/download/16390/23425/); [Duolingo](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/Duolingo_whitepaper_language_video_call_improves_speaking_2025.pdf)

#### Accuracy / lexis plateau
- **[2026]** Intermediate learners using free AI conversation apps improved only in pronunciation, with "minimal change" in lexical and grammatical skills. — [ScienceDirect/IGI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2155709826000137)

### Inferences
- **Never correct from a low-confidence transcript.** Otherwise mis-recognitions get "corrected" as learner errors, which destroys trust.
  - Use ASR or model confidence. When unsure, the tutor should *clarify* ("Did you say …?") rather than correct.
  - Show the transcript so learners can see what was heard.
  - **Test in-house on Russian-accented A2–B1 conversational speech** before launch. Published ASR benchmarks (the L2-ARCTIC L1s) do not include Russian, and conversational learner speech is markedly harder than read speech.
- **Correction policy for prompts:**
  - make minimal edits only;
  - correct at most 1–2 target errors per exchange, prioritising errors that block meaning or match the lesson target;
  - never present stylistic "upgrades" as corrections;
  - never invent rules. If unsure, give the correct form without a rule explanation. This follows from the GEC over-correction evidence and the learner reports of "hallucinated" feedback.
- **Plan for novelty decay.** Expect engagement to drop after the first sessions (Fryer). Counter it with:
  - varied task types;
  - memory and personalisation (see the Praktika vendor claim in Q4);
  - visible progress on speaking-specific metrics;
  - spacing.
- **Train human-like dialogue explicitly** so learners are not trained only for AI-style long-turn exchanges:
  - short turns, interruptions and backchannels, turn-taking phrases;
  - optional bridges to human conversation, e.g. tasks to try with real people.
- **Target accuracy and lexis explicitly.** Free conversation alone plateaus on these for intermediate learners, so the 20-minute structure should include targeted recycling of lesson language and "pushed output" tasks.

### Gaps
- **No published ASR benchmarks for Russian-accented English**, whether for Whisper or for native speech-to-speech models such as Gemini Live and OpenAI Realtime.
- **No systematic study of how accurate LLM metalinguistic (grammar-rule) explanations are for learners.** Evidence is limited to GEC over-correction studies and learner perceptions of "hallucinated" feedback.
- **No speaking studies with delayed post-tests beyond ~1 month** were found.
- **No public retention or engagement curves** for AI speaking apps.
- The Huang, Hew & Fryer (2022, *JCAL* 38(1)) systematic review "Chatbots for language learning—Are they really useful?" is widely cited for technological limitations, the novelty effect and cognitive load, but its specific findings could not be verified in this session. — [Wiley accepted manuscript](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/am-pdf/10.1111/jcal.12610)

## 4. Commercial apps (Speak, Praktika, Duolingo Max Video Call, ELSA Speak, Loora, Talkpal): published efficacy evidence and its quality

### Takeaway
Only **Duolingo** has published a randomized trial of its AI conversation feature (Video Call, 2025). That trial is company-authored and not peer-reviewed, uses an automated test, lasted 30 days, has no delayed post-test, and required heavy usage. **ELSA Speak** and **Talkpal** have only small independent studies in low-tier journals. **Speak, Praktika and Loora** have no published efficacy studies that could be found; their public claims are engagement, satisfaction or marketing figures.

### Cited Findings

#### Duolingo (Max / Video Call with Lily)
- **[2025] [A/V] Whitepaper, "Video Call improves Japanese English learners' speaking skills."**
  - Design: RCT with 567 Japanese speakers learning English at CEFR B1.1, assigned to Video Call (n = 263) or Control (n = 304).
  - Video Call group: ≥2 calls/day for 30 days. Control group: ≥1 regular lesson/day and no calls.
  - Outcome: Versant by Pearson English Speaking and Listening Test, before and after.
  - Results: Video Call "significantly outperformed" Control on speaking and on overall listening+speaking. More words spoken during calls correlated with higher post-test speaking.
  - — [Duolingo whitepaper PDF](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/Duolingo_whitepaper_language_video_call_improves_speaking_2025.pdf); [Duolingo blog: Video Call research report](https://blog.duolingo.com/video-call-research-report)
- **How Duolingo frames it.** The blog says the study "provides the first evidence that Duolingo's Video Call feature can improve English speaking skills for Japanese learners **willing to use the feature regularly**, even after a short period of one month." The snippet also mentions "neutral levels of speaking confidence and willingness to communicate after using Video Call". That wording is ambiguous and may mean confidence and WTC only reached neutral, so verify it. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/video-call-research-report)
- **Quality notes:**
  - vendor-authored whitepaper, not peer-reviewed;
  - effect size and attrition not captured (the PDF returned HTTP 403);
  - single L1 and single level;
  - automated outcome test;
  - no delayed post-test;
  - control condition not clearly time-matched;

- **[2024] [V] Earlier Duolingo whitepapers (not specific to Video Call):**
  - "Duolingo learners can start a conversation after 4–6 weeks of app use" (A1 level) — [PDF](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/Duolingo_whitepaper_language_conversation_2024.pdf)

  - This illustrates why conversation features are being added to such apps.
  - — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341904580_The_effectiveness_of_app-based_language_instruction_for_developing_receptive_linguistic_knowledge_and_oral_communicative_ability)

#### Loora
- No independent or company efficacy study was found; only review and marketing pages. — [icanlearn review](https://www.icanlearn.com/loora-ai/)

### Inferences
- **Efficacy evidence in this category is thin.** Apart from Duolingo's single RCT, vendor "efficacy" means engagement, retention or satisfaction. An externally validated study would be a genuine differentiator and a defensible marketing claim if it used human-rated speaking, a delayed post-test and an A2–B1 Russian-L1 sample.
- **Duolingo's trial is the closest benchmark.** B1 learners who did ~2 short AI calls a day for 30 days showed measurable gains on a standardised automated speaking test. Replicating this design with Russian-L1 learners, and adding a delayed post-test and human raters, is a sensible internal validation template.
- **Memory and personalisation are a plausible engagement lever** (Praktika's +24% Day-1 retention claim). Engagement is not learning, so measure both.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes, attrition and full confidence/WTC statistics for the Duolingo RCT were not retrieved (PDF access blocked).
- Speak, ELSA and Praktika have no public internal efficacy reports; Loora has no data at all.
- A randomized ELSA study with 60 Iranian high-school learners (30/30) appeared in search results, but its source could not be pinned down.

## 5. Transparency norms (disclosing that the tutor is AI) and learner attitudes to AI tutors

### Takeaway
From **2 August 2026 the EU AI Act (Art. 50)** requires that people be told they are interacting with an AI system, at the latest at first interaction, unless this is obvious. Learners broadly welcome AI speaking partners as a **low-stakes, non-judgmental** space, which is the mechanism behind the anxiety and WTC gains. They still prefer humans for authenticity and trust teacher feedback more. Disclosure research outside education shows mixed but mostly small effects, with usefulness tending to dominate.

### Cited Findings

#### Regulation
- **[2026] EU AI Act, Article 50 — applies from 2 August 2026.**
  - Providers must design AI systems that interact directly with natural persons (chatbots, AI agents, avatars) so that people are informed they are interacting with AI.
  - The exception is where this is "obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect."
  - The information must be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" and must meet accessibility requirements.
  - — [AI Act Art. 50 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [European Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/is-it-a-bot-eu-ai-act-transparency-rules-take-effect-2-august-2026/)
- The European Commission has published guidelines on Art. 50 transparency obligations (content not extracted). — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations)

#### Learner attitudes toward AI speaking partners
- **[2025–2026] Anxiety and comfort.** Participants reported "reduced task anxiety and increased comfort with the AI partner as compared with human interlocutors." AI partners "reduce perceived social threat by offering private, low-stakes practice opportunities." *(attribution uncertain — blended summary of 2025–2026 perception studies)* — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025, AI chatbots vs human facilitation in EFL speaking assessment](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1745942/full); [PMC 2026 qualitative study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13050954/)
- **[2025–2026] Authenticity and trust.** "Human interlocutors were consistently preferred for their authentic nature." Credibility was anchored in teacher assessment, and AI feedback was sometimes seen as "irrelevant or hallucinated." *(attribution uncertain)* — [TESOL Union PDF](https://www.tesolunion.org/attachments/files/4ZTU2DNGI1BMMJK7MGVIDN2JKEZJNKDN2M37NJK27MGVJ6ZDHI4ZJQ0CMMVMDNDKZ3YZY57YTKY4NDFM7YJYX6M2MZ4LJQ24NZG2ENDAW4LJKZ.pdf)
- **[2026]** Attitudes toward AI *improved* after a real-time AI speaking task (78 German-L2 learners). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22225)
- **[2026, CHI]** In Indian schools there was high demand across students, teachers and other stakeholders, and students' speaking confidence rose. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791266)
- **[2025, System]** Learners valued the chatbot's "accessibility, authenticity, and multimodal features." *(attribution uncertain)* — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X2500329X)

#### Effects of AI-identity disclosure (non-education contexts; all *attribution uncertain*, from a blended search summary)
- Disclosing a chatbot's identity up front led to lower perceived social presence, trust and attitude toward an online retailer than not disclosing it. — [Springer: "Should a Chatbot Disclose Itself? Implications for an Online Conversational Retailer"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-68288-0_1)
- In customer-service settings, disclosure "did not necessarily undermine social presence or reduce helpfulness," because users prioritised perceived usefulness. — [academia.edu: "Trust me, I'm a bot"](https://www.academia.edu/55501599/Trust_me_Im_a_bot_Repercussions_of_chatbot_disclosure_in_different_service_frontline_settings)
- In a crowdfunding context, AI disclosure had a direct positive effect on engagement intention but could lower it indirectly through reduced perceived content quality. — [arXiv 2602.15698](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15698)
- A cross-national study's title states that humanlike AI design "Increases Anthropomorphism but Yields Divergent Outcomes on Engagement and Trust Globally" (details not extracted). — [arXiv 2512.17898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17898)

### Inferences
- **Disclose on the first screen and in the first spoken line** (e.g., "I'm your AI speaking partner").
  - This is legally required for EU users from 2 Aug 2026 and good practice everywhere.
  - It also supports the pedagogical mechanism: learners feel safe *because* the partner is not a human judge.
  - Disclosure is unlikely to cost much in a product valued for usefulness.
- **A warm, human-like persona is compatible with disclosure.** A named, consistent voice character can be clearly labelled as AI. The avatar/WTC evidence (System 2024) suggests human-likeness helps affect, while the disclosure literature suggests usefulness is what sustains trust.
- **Be honest about feedback authority.** Learners trust teacher feedback more and notice "hallucinated" AI feedback. The tutor should hedge uncertain corrections and avoid confident rule statements it cannot back.

### Gaps
- Russian-law requirements on AI disclosure were not researched in this session.
- No experimental study was found on how **AI-identity disclosure affects L2 speaking anxiety or WTC** specifically.
- No study was found on attitudes to AI tutors among **Russian-speaking adult learners**.
